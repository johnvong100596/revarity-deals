The proposal

The operator and the tool shouldn't share a face.

Right now everything sits under one brand. That's fine for the operator business. It's a ceiling for the SaaS — because the moment Revarity recommends a tool that's also stamped "Revarity," it reads as self-promotion, and no competitor will ever distribute a product that feeds a rival.

The reference is the Yaman model: a separate CRM SaaS brand run alongside the main company. The separation is what lets the main company vouch for the tool credibly, and lets the tool stand on its own. David's read is the same, and it's right.

Why it's the right call

Three things separation buys.

01

We can vouch without bias

A distinct brand lets Revarity recommend the tool as a real product, not a house ad. Credibility we can't manufacture under one name.

02

Competitors become distributors

Other Airbnb operators will run and resell a neutral tool. An affiliate channel built from the very people who'd never promote anything labeled Revarity.

03

The operator brand stays clean

The SaaS lives or dies on its own metrics without dragging — or being dragged by — the operator brand. Two stories, two audiences, no cross-contamination.

The honest cost — this is a build, not a rename

Separation breaks the locked all-*.revarity.com architecture. A new brand needs its own domain and wildcard cert, its own Meta ad account, its own funnel and creative set, its own identity, and its own support surface. Budget it as real work, not a label swap.

The options

Three ways to play it.

A

Keep it under Revarity

Status quo. No new domain, no new build. Everything stays one brand.

Trade: self-serving optics, and the affiliate channel is dead on arrival. We cap the SaaS to grow only what the operator brand can reach.
B

Fully separate — brand, funnel, and backend

A clean-room second company: its own everything, including a separate technical stack.

Trade: maximum independence, maximum cost and duplication. We'd rebuild the spine and governance we already have, for no real gain.
C

Separate face, shared spine Recommended

A distinct brand, domain, ad account, and funnel up front — but the same Postgres spine, governance layer, and connector-edge architecture behind it. Customers and competitors see an independent product; we run one backend.

Trade: requires clean brand/data boundaries so the separation reads as real. This is exactly the connector-edge posture — customers bring their own credentials; we supply the intelligence — so it fits what's already being built.
Recommendation

Take C. Build the separate brand, domain, and funnel — but keep the shared spine and governance behind it. It captures the affiliate upside and the credibility, without paying to duplicate infrastructure we already own.

Decision needed before any SaaS-facing surface is built

This does not block the STR-operator funnel, the Creative Engine, or the lead magnets — build those now regardless. It only gates the SaaS brand, domain, and affiliate work.
